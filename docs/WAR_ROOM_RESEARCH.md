# War Room: research and product decisions

Research checked 23 November 2026. The War Room is a local prototype for
examining one completed synthetic Survival Lab experiment. Its premise is a
research hypothesis: a strategy reviewer may find it useful to inspect a
specific disagreement and its supporting records, rather than just a chart.

## What the research suggests

- [Anthropic's guide to building agents](https://www.anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents)
  distinguishes predefined workflows from autonomous agents and recommends
  starting simply and grounding decisions in observations. AXIOM uses a
  bounded review workflow: it reruns a known scenario, presents recorded
  evidence, and does not give the reviewers tools to trade or browse.
- [Research on multi-agent debate](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.08788) warns
  that evaluation practices make broad conclusions difficult. A panel of
  reviewers is not proof of accuracy, independence, or improvement over a
  single reviewer. The three Claude perspectives use the same configured
  model and an auditor can see the earlier two outputs.
- [NIST's Generative AI Profile](https://nvlpubs.nist.gov/nistpubs/ai/NIST.AI.600-1.pdf)
  describes confabulation and risks of overreliance. A model can cite an
  existing record while still misinterpreting it. AXIOM checks whether cited
  identifiers exist; this is not a factual verification of model prose.
- [Alpaca's paper-trading documentation](https://docs.alpaca.markets/us/docs/paper-trading)
  describes execution differences such as market impact and queue position.
  Survival Lab is more limited still: it generates artificial daily prices
  and assumes next-open fills and unlimited liquidity. It is not a live
  paper-trading feed or evidence of performance in actual markets.
- [C2PA's provenance explainer](https://spec.c2pa.org/specifications/specifications/2.4/explainer/Explainer.html)
  distinguishes provenance from truth. AXIOM's fingerprint is not a signed
  provenance record: it hashes the request, generated candles and declared
  engine metadata, not the source code, output authenticity or execution.

## Existing products and the proposed difference

[QuantConnect](https://www.quantconnect.com/docs/v2/cloud-platform/backtesting/results)
already provides backtest results and trade records;
[Composer](https://help.composer.trade/article/67-backtest-basics) offers
visual backtesting; and [TradingAgents](https://github.com/TauricResearch/TradingAgents)
already explores specialized model roles in trading. AXIOM does not invent
backtesting or multi-agent debate. Its unvalidated product idea is a focused
review flow that takes a question to the exact recorded decision, assumption
or metric, with the human retaining responsibility for the interpretation.

## Prototype boundaries

The **offline walkthrough** is generated by rules from the recorded run. It
uses no AI. The optional **Claude perspectives** send a compact set of
generated synthetic evidence to Anthropic and make three model requests;
they require an explicitly configured server-side API key and may incur
charges. Claude Code's signed-in account is for development and is not used
as an application API credential. See [Anthropic's API documentation](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/api/messages/create)
and [Claude Code's authentication guidance](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/legal-and-compliance).
The local process limits the number of paid review attempts per launch; it is
not a persistent billing or security control for a shared deployment.

The review reruns the selected simulation and checks its fingerprint against
the last completed experiment. It cannot establish whether a strategy is
profitable, safe, or suitable for investment. The buy-pause guard does not
force a sale or guarantee a loss limit, and the buy-and-hold comparison is
not exposure-matched. The content is for exploration, not trading advice.

## How to evaluate the hypothesis

With permission and suitable data, ask strategy developers and reviewers to
investigate the same preselected cases using (1) recorded results alone,
(2) a single model, and (3) the three-perspective workflow. Counterbalance
order, use a prespecified rubric and an independent evaluator where possible,
and record missed or invented findings, inspection time, disagreements,
model cost, and whether the review changes a real decision. Include cases
with no guard, no trigger, a rejected or expired order, and different paths.
Do not infer product value or accuracy from a compelling animation or a
hand-picked synthetic example.

Before any real-market claim, use appropriately licensed historical data,
independent and out-of-sample evaluation, realistic execution assumptions,
and a security/privacy review. Product demand, pricing and the value of the
three-perspective design remain to be tested.
